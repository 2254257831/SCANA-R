THE RESEARCH QUESTION

Can calibrated perturbations produce useful recovery data?

Successful demonstrations sparsely cover the states encountered after a policy deviates from its reference trajectory. SCANA-R uses source-episode cross-fitting to estimate policy errors, samples a short perturbation from the same task and nearby phase, and executes it in simulation. The reference action sequence then resumes. A branch is accepted only when its terminal state satisfies the task’s success criterion.

Recovery supervision pairs newly observed states with actions that were actually executed. With a matched final training budget, this improves success from 67% to 92% on Transfer Cube and from 43% to 67% on Insertion. The study uses current privileged state in two ACT simulation tasks; visual policies and real robots remain outside the present evaluation.

HOW IT WORKS

Three stages, one consistent training record

Full protocol ↗
01

Calibrate by source episode

Five auxiliary models predict action chunks for held-out source episodes. Calibration errors retain the task and temporal phase of their source.

02

Execute and verify

An eight-step joint pulse is bounded and gripper-protected. Failed branches retry with a smaller pulse; acceptance requires terminal task success.

03

Train on observed recovery

After the pulse, record actual observations and executed reference actions. Mix recovery and original data in equal proportions.

Where calibration helps

Against Gaussian recovery, SCANA-R gains 22 points on Insertion (paired 95% interval: 5.0 to 40.5). Transfer Cube gains 4.5 points with an interval spanning zero (−5.0 to 16.0). The current evidence supports a task-dependent benefit, rather than uniform superiority.

Intervals use paired resampling of both seeds and layouts. Five policy fits are not 200 independent training runs. Inspect all paired comparisons ↗

Historical method diagnostics and evidence limits

The complete CSV includes four historical label-only controls, bringing the total to 3,200 rollouts. Their original checkpoints and sampling streams are retained; these comparisons diagnose earlier versions and are not single-component ablations. No same-collection-budget DART reproduction is included. One recovery bank is shared across final training seeds. Read the evidence scope.

Scope of the current study

SCANA-R is evaluated with current privileged simulator state on two bimanual tasks. Recovery collection requires simulator resets, a reference action sequence, auxiliary-policy training and additional rollouts. The present experiments do not establish visual-policy transfer, real-robot performance or general collision safety. Recovery collection is related to DART; the results isolate the comparisons documented above.
